Know before you burn

Inspection levels, explained plainly

The national standard (NFPA 211) defines three levels of chimney inspection. We'll tell you which one you need and why, so you never pay for one you don't.

Annual check-up

Best for: yearly maintenance, no changes to your system

A visual check of the readily accessible parts of the chimney, flue and appliance. It comes free with every sweep.

  • Firebox, damper & smoke chamber
  • Creosote level & blockages
  • Exterior crown, cap & brick
  • Clearances to combustibles

Camera inspection

Best for: buying/selling a home, after a chimney fire, storm or new appliance

Everything in Level 1, plus an HD video scan of the whole flue and access to attics and crawlspaces. Required by NFPA 211 at property transfer.

  • Full-length HD flue video
  • Cracked tiles & liner gaps
  • Attic & roofline check
  • Realtor-ready written report

Concealed-area inspection

Best for: when a Level 2 points to a hidden hazard

Only needed when serious damage is suspected behind walls or masonry. Some components may need removal to access. We'll always explain why before recommending it.

  • Access to concealed areas
  • Structural assessment
  • Detailed repair scope
  • Rarely needed, never upsold

How often should my chimney be swept?
The NFPA recommends an inspection every year, with cleaning as needed. Most wood-burning fireplaces used regularly need a sweep annually. Gas fireplaces still need an annual inspection, since nests, debris and deteriorating liners can cause carbon monoxide problems.
How long does a sweep take?
Usually 45–90 minutes for a standard sweep and Level 1 inspection. Camera inspections and multi-flue chimneys take a little longer. We'll give you an estimate when you book.
Will it make a mess in my house?
No. We lay drop cloths from the door to the fireplace, wear shoe covers, and run a HEPA vacuum system that keeps soot under negative pressure the whole time. You shouldn't be able to tell we were there, except your chimney will be clean.
Do I need to do anything before you arrive?
Please don't use the fireplace for 24 hours beforehand so the flue is cool. Clear a path to the fireplace and move fragile items off the mantel. That's it.
I'm buying a house. Do I need a Level 2 inspection?
NFPA 211 calls for a Level 2 inspection on property sale or transfer. Standard home inspectors don't inspect the inside of the flue, so a camera inspection is the only way to know its condition before you close. We can usually schedule within a few days and provide a realtor-ready report.
Do you give free estimates on repairs?
Yes. If we find something during a sweep or inspection, you'll get a written, itemized estimate with photos at no extra charge and no pressure to decide on the spot.
Do you service gas fireplaces, furnace flues and wood stoves?
Yes: wood-burning fireplaces, inserts, wood and pellet stoves, gas fireplaces and logs, and oil or gas furnace and water heater flues.

Chimneys in Peters Township

Newer homes, different chimneys

Peters Township sits just over the Allegheny County line in northern Washington County. It has some older farmhouses and mid-century homes, but much of the township was built from the 1970s onward, and a lot of it in the 1990s and 2000s as subdivisions spread along the Route 19 corridor.

Homes from that era often have a factory-built (prefab) fireplace: a metal firebox and a metal chimney pipe, boxed in by a wood-framed chase with siding to match the house. At the top of the chase is a sheet-metal chase cover.

That chase cover is the weak spot. Cheaper galvanized covers rust, and many are flat, so water pools on them. Over time the rust spreads, water drips down inside the chase, and you see stains on the ceiling or the wall beside the fireplace, or rot in the framing. Replacing the cover with a sloped stainless one before it fails is far cheaper than fixing a rotted chase.

Common questions

Does a prefab fireplace need sweeping?
Yes, if you burn wood in it. Creosote builds up in a metal flue just like a masonry one. Factory-built systems also need regular inspection of the firebox panels, the flue sections and the chase cover, since cracked panels or a rusted cover can lead to bigger problems.
How do I know if my chase cover is rusting?
Rust streaks running down the siding on the chase are the usual giveaway. Inside, look for water stains on the ceiling or wall near the fireplace, or rust on the firebox top. We'll photograph the cover up close so you can see it for yourself.
Do you service direct-vent gas fireplaces?
Yes. Sealed gas fireplaces with a glass front still need an annual check: glass and gasket, burner and logs, pilot, and the vent termination outside, which can get blocked by nests or plants.
Can you inspect before we close on a house in Peters Township?
Yes. A Level 2 inspection includes a camera look inside the flue and a check of the chase or chimney exterior, and is what NFPA 211 calls for at a sale. See our real estate inspections.

Chimneys in Wexford

One ZIP code, several townships

Wexford isn't a municipality of its own. It's a mailing address that covers neighborhoods in several North Hills townships, including parts of Pine, Marshall, Franklin Park and McCandless. If your address says Wexford, we cover you.

Much of the area was built up from the 1980s through the 2000s along the Route 19 and I-79 corridor, with larger two-story homes on generous, often wooded lots. You'll also find older houses along the main roads and newer construction still going up.

Chimneys here are a real mix. Some homes have a full masonry chimney. Others have a factory-built fireplace in a chase finished with siding or stone veneer, which can look like masonry from the street. And a lot of homeowners have added a wood-burning insert or a freestanding stove to get real heat out of the fireplace.

Masonry fireplaces

Clay-lined brick chimneys. Watch the crown, the mortar near the top and the flashing where it meets the roof.

Factory-built

Metal flue in a framed chase. Needs the right parts for the model, and a sound chase cover on top.

Inserts & stoves

Efficient heat, but they burn cooler in the flue and can build creosote faster if the venting isn't right.

Burning wood in the North Hills

Inserts need a proper liner

A fireplace insert is a stove that slides into your existing fireplace. Older installations often just pushed the insert in and ran a short pipe up past the damper, leaving the rest of the masonry flue far too large for it. The exhaust cools, creosote builds quickly, and the insert can draft poorly.

Current standards call for an insert to be connected directly to the flue, and a full-length stainless steel liner sized to the insert is the usual way to do it. It's also much easier to sweep.

The three stages of creosote

Good burning habits

  • Burn seasoned wood. Split, dried and stored under cover. Wet wood smolders and makes more creosote.
  • Don't choke the fire. Running an insert or stove damped down all night is the fastest way to build creosote.
  • Sweep on schedule. If you heat with wood, have it checked every year, and more often if you burn a lot.
  • Keep the cap on. It keeps rain, leaves and animals out of the flue.

How often should a wood insert be swept?
At least once a year if you use it regularly, and more often if you burn a lot or notice heavy buildup. The NFPA standard is to inspect annually and sweep when deposits build up. We'll tell you what we found and recommend a schedule.
Our chimney looks like stone. Is it masonry or factory-built?
It could be either. Many newer homes have a framed chase finished with stone veneer. Look inside the firebox: brick usually means masonry, and metal or smooth panels usually mean factory-built. We'll confirm it on our first visit.
Can you inspect a chimney before we close on a house in Wexford?
Yes. We do Level 2 camera inspections for buyers and sellers, with a written report. See our real estate inspections.

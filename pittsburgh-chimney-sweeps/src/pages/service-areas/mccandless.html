Chimneys in McCandless

Mid-century homes, now on their second or third furnace

The Town of McCandless sits in the North Hills along Route 19, next to North Park. Most of its neighborhoods were built in the decades after the Second World War: brick and frame ranches, split-levels and two-story colonials on quiet, hilly streets, with newer development mixed in.

A typical chimney here is brick with clay-tile flues, one for a fireplace and one for the gas furnace and water heater. The brick has seen 50 or more Pittsburgh winters, so we check the crown and the top courses of mortar closely. But the issue we most want McCandless homeowners to know about is inside the furnace flue.

The orphaned water heater

When the furnace leaves the chimney

Older gas furnaces and water heaters usually shared one flue. When the furnace is replaced with a high-efficiency model, the new one vents out the side of the house through plastic pipe. The water heater is left behind, alone in a flue that was sized for both.

A water heater on its own doesn't make enough warm exhaust to heat a big masonry flue. The gases cool, moisture condenses on the flue walls, and the draft gets weak. Over time that acidic moisture eats at clay tiles and mortar, and in a bad case exhaust can spill back into the basement.

The fix is usually a stainless steel liner sized for the water heater alone. If you've replaced your furnace in the last 20 years or so and nobody looked at the chimney, it's worth a check.

Do you service gas furnace and water heater flues in McCandless?
Yes. We inspect the appliance flue for blockages, nests, fallen tile and condensation damage, and check whether it's sized correctly for what's venting into it now. If it isn't, we'll explain the options and give you a written price.
How do I know if my water heater is orphaned?
If your furnace vents out the side of the house through white plastic pipe, but your water heater still connects to the chimney, it's probably venting alone. Rust or white staining near the water heater's vent connection, or water at the bottom of the chimney, are other clues.
Can you inspect before we close on a house in McCandless?
Yes. NFPA 211 calls for a Level 2 inspection when a home changes hands, and it's a good time to catch appliance venting problems. See our real estate inspections.

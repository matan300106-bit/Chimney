Chimneys in Monroeville

A post-war suburb that runs on gas

Monroeville grew fast in the decades after the Second World War, helped by the Pennsylvania Turnpike and the Route 22 corridor. Much of its housing dates from the 1950s through the 1970s: brick ranches, split-levels and two-story homes, plus apartment, condo and townhome communities and newer houses built since.

Most of these homes heat with natural gas, and the chimney usually does double duty: a fireplace flue and a flue for the gas furnace and water heater. Over the years, many owners have stopped burning wood and put in gas logs or a gas insert instead.

That's a reasonable choice, but it isn't plug-and-play. A wood flue that's being converted needs to be clean and sound first. Vented gas logs need the damper fixed open. A gas insert needs its own vent liner. And the furnace flue still needs checking every year, because gas exhaust is full of water vapor that's hard on old clay tile.

Thinking of going gas?

Three ways to convert a wood fireplace

Each one suits a different goal. We'll inspect your fireplace and flue first, then help you choose.

Looks

Vented gas logs

Realistic flames in your existing fireplace. Mostly decorative: much of the heat goes up the chimney. The damper must stay open whenever they're in use.

Heat

Direct-vent gas insert

A sealed unit with a glass front that fits into the firebox, vented through liners run up the chimney. Much more efficient, and it closes off the open fireplace.

Keep wood

Wood insert instead

If you'd rather keep burning wood, a wood insert with a full stainless liner gives far more heat than an open fireplace.

Gas fireplaces still make carbon monoxideNever run vented gas logs with the damper closed. Keep CO alarms on every level of your home, and have gas appliances and their flues checked every year.

Monroeville FAQ

Gas and chimney questions

Not sure what you have? Call or text {{phone}} and describe it. We'll help.

Do you service gas furnace flues in Monroeville?
Yes. We check the furnace and water heater flue for blockages, nests, fallen tile and moisture damage, and confirm it's the right size for what's venting into it. Gas exhaust carries a lot of water vapor, so older clay-lined flues are worth checking every year.
Can I put gas logs in my old wood fireplace?
Usually, yes, if the firebox and flue are in good shape. The flue should be swept and inspected first, and vented gas logs need the damper permanently held open. We'll check it and tell you whether logs or an insert is the better fit.
Do gas fireplaces need a chimney sweep?
They don't make creosote, but they still need an annual inspection and cleaning. Nests, debris, deteriorating liners and dirty burners can all cause problems, including carbon monoxide.

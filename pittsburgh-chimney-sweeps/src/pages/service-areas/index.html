By part of town

Different neighborhoods, different chimneys

A 1920s brick house in the South Hills and a 2005 townhouse in Cranberry need different things. Here's how the region breaks down.

City of Pittsburgh

Rowhouses, attached homes and hillside houses, many over a century old. Shared chimney stacks, old coal-era fireplaces and tricky access on steep streets.

  • Pittsburgh city neighborhoods

South Hills

Early-1900s brick and stone in Mt. Lebanon, post-war ranches and split-levels farther out, and newer subdivisions in Peters Township.

  • Mt. Lebanon
  • Upper St. Clair
  • Bethel Park
  • Peters Township

North Hills

Mid-century homes close to the city, and plenty of newer construction along the Route 19 and I-79 corridor, where factory-built fireplaces are common.

  • Ross Township
  • McCandless
  • Wexford
  • Cranberry Township

East & Allegheny Valley

Large wooded lots and multi-flue chimneys in Fox Chapel, gas-heated suburban homes in Monroeville, and wood stoves on bigger properties in Murrysville.

  • Fox Chapel
  • Monroeville
  • Murrysville

West, Airport & Ohio River

Historic river-town homes in Sewickley, and suburban neighborhoods and townhomes around Moon and Robinson near the airport corridor.

  • Sewickley
  • Moon Township
  • Robinson Township

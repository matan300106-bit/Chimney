Chimneys in Sewickley

An old river town, and old chimneys to match

Sewickley is a borough on the Ohio River, northwest of the city. Its village center and surrounding streets are known for houses from the late 1800s and early 1900s, many of them Victorian, alongside larger homes in the hills of Sewickley Heights and neighboring Edgeworth.

Chimneys on homes of this age were often built with softer brick and lime-based mortar. That combination is meant to flex a little and let moisture escape through the joints. When someone later repoints with a hard, modern cement mortar, the mortar can end up stronger than the brick. Water gets trapped, and the faces of the bricks start to pop off in winter. It's a common problem on older homes, and it's avoidable.

Older fireplaces here may have been built for coal, with small fireboxes and decorative surrounds, and many flues are unlined or lined with clay tile that has had a century to crack. Some homes have had their fireplaces closed for decades. Before you light one again, it's worth a proper look inside.

Keeping the look of an older chimneyA good repair should be hard to spot. We match mortar color and joint style as closely as we can, reuse sound original brick where possible, and recommend copper caps where they suit the house.

Sewickley FAQ

Older-home questions

More answers on our FAQ page.

Our fireplace hasn't been used in years. Is it safe to light?
Don't assume it is. Closed-up fireplaces often have nests, debris, a stuck damper or cracked flue tiles. A camera inspection tells you whether it's ready to use, needs cleaning, or needs a liner or repair first.
Why does the mortar used on an old chimney matter?
Older brick is often softer than modern brick. Mortar that's harder than the brick holds water in and forces the damage into the brick faces, which then flake or pop off in freeze-thaw weather. Using a softer, compatible mortar lets the joints do their job and protects the brick.
Do you install copper chimney caps?
Yes. We install copper and stainless steel caps, including multi-flue caps. Copper is a popular choice on older homes because it weathers to a darker patina that suits old brick and stone.
Can you inspect a chimney before we close on a house in Sewickley?
Yes. NFPA 211 calls for a Level 2 inspection when a home is sold. On older houses it's especially useful, since a standard home inspection usually doesn't look inside the flue. See our real estate inspections.

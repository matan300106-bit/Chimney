Chimneys in Ross Township

Close to the city, built on the slopes

Ross is one of the closest North Hills townships to the city, stretching north from the city line along Route 19 and McKnight Road. Its housing spans most of the last century: older homes in the neighborhoods nearest Pittsburgh, and lots of post-war ranches, Cape Cods and two-story brick homes farther out.

What ties it together is the terrain. Streets climb and drop, many lots are steep, and houses are often set into the hillside. A chimney might clear the roof by a few feet on the uphill side and stand two stories tall on the downhill side.

Terrain affects how a chimney works. A house below a ridge, or with tall trees nearby, can sit in turbulent air that pushes smoke back down the flue. A tall, exposed stack takes more wind-driven rain, so its brick and crown wear faster. And on a steep lot, simply setting a ladder safely takes planning.

Signs of a draft problem

  • Smoke drifts into the room when you light a fire, or when the wind picks up
  • A smoky or tar-like smell from the fireplace on humid summer days
  • The fire is hard to start or won't stay lit
  • Cold air pouring out of the fireplace when it isn't in use
  • Soot stains on the face of the fireplace above the opening

The cause can be the flue, the chimney's height, the cap, the house itself, or the hill around it. We look at all of them before recommending anything.

Ross Township FAQ

Hillside chimney questions

More answers on our FAQ page.

Why does my fireplace smell in the summer?
Creosote in the flue gives off a smell when the air is warm and humid. It's often worse when the house pulls air down the chimney, for example when exhaust fans or the AC are running. A sweep removes most of the source, and a cap and a top-sealing damper help keep outside air from coming down.
Will a new chimney cap stop the downdrafts?
Sometimes. Wind-directional caps can help when wind gusting over the roof or hill is pushing air down the flue. They won't fix a flue that's too small, a chimney that's too short, or a house that's starved for air, so we diagnose before we sell you a cap.
Can you work on a chimney on a steep lot?
Yes. Steep yards, retaining walls and tall downhill chimneys are routine for us. Mention it when you book so we bring the right ladders and equipment.

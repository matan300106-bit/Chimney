Chimneys in Robinson Township

Hilltops, wind and water

Robinson Township sits west of the city along I-376 and Steubenville Pike, best known for its big shopping areas. Away from the retail corridors, it's mostly residential: mid-century neighborhoods, newer subdivisions and townhome communities spread over rolling hills.

Older homes here usually have a brick chimney with a clay liner. Newer ones often have a factory-built fireplace in a sided chase, or a direct-vent gas fireplace. Either way, homes on higher, open ground take more wind, and wind drives rain sideways into brick, under flashing and through gaps in chase covers.

Chimney leaks are often blamed on the roof, and roof repairs often don't fix them. Water can enter at the crown, through porous brick, at the flashing, through an uncapped flue, or at a rusted chase cover. Finding the actual source is most of the job.

Signs of a chimney leak

What to watch for

  • Stains or bubbling paint on the ceiling or wall beside the chimney
  • Water or rust in the firebox, or on the damper
  • A musty smell from the fireplace after rain
  • White, chalky staining (efflorescence) on the brick outside
  • Brick faces flaking or popping off after winter

We check each possible entry point, photograph what we find, and give you a written repair price. Repair estimates are free.

Robinson FAQ

Leak and repair questions

Seeing water right now? Call or text {{phone}}.

My roofer fixed the roof but the chimney still leaks. Why?
Because the water is probably coming in through the chimney itself: a cracked crown, porous brick, a missing cap, or flashing that was reused instead of replaced. We test each one and show you photos of where the water is getting in.
Is waterproofing a chimney worth it?
On sound brick that soaks up water, a breathable water repellent can help a lot. It lets moisture out while keeping rain from soaking in. It isn't a fix for cracked crowns, open mortar joints or bad flashing, so those come first.
Our newer home has a sided chimney chase. Can that leak too?
Yes. The metal chase cover on top can rust or hold water, and siding joints around the chase can let water in. A sloped stainless chase cover is the usual long-term fix.

Built for Pittsburgh homes

Hills, old brick and hard winters

Pittsburgh chimneys take a beating. A lot of the housing stock here is older brick, and our winters swing above and below freezing again and again. Water gets into mortar, freezes, expands, and slowly pushes brick apart.

  • Freeze-thaw damage. We look for spalling brick, cracked crowns and open mortar joints before they turn into a rebuild.
  • Older clay-tile flues. Many older homes have clay liners that crack with age. Our camera shows you exactly what's inside.
  • Steep roofs and hillside lots. Our crews are equipped and insured for tall chimneys and tricky access.
  • Furnace and water-heater flues. Gas appliances vent through chimneys too, and a blocked flue is a carbon monoxide risk.

How often should my chimney be swept?
The NFPA recommends an inspection every year, with cleaning as needed. Most wood-burning fireplaces used regularly need a sweep annually. Gas fireplaces still need an annual inspection, since nests, debris and deteriorating liners can cause carbon monoxide problems.
How long does a sweep take?
Usually 45–90 minutes for a standard sweep and Level 1 inspection. Camera inspections and multi-flue chimneys take a little longer. We'll give you an estimate when you book.
Will it make a mess in my house?
No. We lay drop cloths from the door to the fireplace, wear shoe covers, and run a HEPA vacuum system that keeps soot under negative pressure the whole time.
I'm buying a house. Do I need a Level 2 inspection?
NFPA 211 calls for a Level 2 inspection on property sale or transfer. Standard home inspectors don't inspect the inside of the flue, so a camera inspection is the only way to know its condition before you close.
Do you give free estimates on repairs?
Yes. If we find something during a sweep or inspection, you'll get a written, itemized estimate with photos at no extra charge and no pressure to decide on the spot.

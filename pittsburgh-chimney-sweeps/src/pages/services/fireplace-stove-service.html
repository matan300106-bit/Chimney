What we service

Four kinds of appliance, one visit

Every appliance has its own wear points. Here's what we look at for each, on top of the chimney or vent it connects to.

Dampers

The damper closes the flue when the fireplace isn't in use. A rusted, warped or stuck damper wastes heated air all winter, or won't open fully and lets smoke into the room.

  • Throat damper cleaned, freed up and adjusted
  • Handle, pivots and seal checked
  • Top-sealing damper installed when the old one can't be saved

Gas fireplaces and gas logs

Gas fireplaces don't make creosote, but they still need yearly attention. Burners, pilot assemblies and vents collect dust, debris and the occasional nest.

  • Burner, pilot and logs cleaned; logs placed per the manufacturer's diagram
  • Glass cleaned and gasket checked on sealed units
  • Vent or chimney checked for blockage, and the termination outside
  • Damper clamp checked on vented log sets in a masonry fireplace

Fireplace inserts

An insert turns an open fireplace into an efficient heater. It also changes how the chimney works, so it needs a properly connected liner and regular cleaning.

  • Insert, baffle and liner swept
  • Liner connection and top plate checked
  • Door gaskets, glass and surround inspected

Wood and pellet stoves

Stoves burn hotter and longer than open fireplaces, and wood stoves used for heat can build creosote quickly. Pellet stoves have a small, fan-driven exhaust path that clogs with fine ash.

  • Stove pipe, flue and cap cleaned
  • Baffles, door gaskets and glass checked
  • Pellet stove exhaust path, vent pipe and ash areas cleaned
  • Clearances to walls and floor checked
Dampers, explained

Throat damper or top-sealing damper?

Most older fireplaces have a throat damper: a cast-iron plate just above the firebox, opened and closed with a handle or lever. It sits in heat, soot and any water that comes down the flue, so over decades it rusts, warps or seizes. Even a working one rarely seals tightly.

A top-sealing damper mounts at the top of the chimney and closes with a gasketed lid, operated by a cable that runs down to a handle in the firebox. Because it seals at the top, it also keeps cold or humid outside air from sitting in the flue when you're not burning.

If your throat damper can be cleaned and adjusted, we'll do that. If it can't, a top-sealing damper is usually the better replacement, and a written price comes before any work.

Signs it needs service

What to watch for

  • The damper is stuck, won't close, or you feel a cold draft from the fireplace
  • Gas fireplace pilot won't stay lit, or the flames look lazy, tall or yellow and sooty
  • Black soot on gas logs, the glass or the surround
  • Smoke spills into the room when you open the stove door to reload
  • Stove glass blackens quickly, or the door gasket no longer seals
  • Pellet stove smells of smoke, shows error codes or the fire is sluggish
  • A new insert or stove was installed and the chimney was never checked

Does a gas fireplace really need yearly service?
Yes. NFPA 211 calls for annual inspection of gas venting, and most gas fireplace manufacturers recommend yearly service. Dust and debris build up on burners and pilots, log sets shift, and vents can be blocked by nests or debris, which can send exhaust, including carbon monoxide, back into the room.
Why is there soot on my gas logs or glass?
Usually the logs have moved out of position, so the flame hits them and burns incompletely, or the burner ports and air openings are partly blocked. It's a sign the fireplace isn't burning cleanly. Stop using it until it's been serviced, and don't rearrange the logs except to match the manufacturer's placement diagram.
Can I burn wood in a fireplace that has gas logs?
Not with the gas logs in place. Gas log sets aren't designed to have wood burned on them. If you want to go back to wood, the gas logs need to be removed and the gas safely capped, and the chimney should be swept and inspected before your first wood fire.
Should I replace my old throat damper?
Only if it can't be made to work. Many stuck throat dampers can be cleaned and adjusted. If the plate is cracked, badly rusted or warped, a top-sealing damper is usually the better replacement because it seals more tightly and keeps outside air out of the flue.
How often should a pellet stove be cleaned?
Follow your stove's manual for routine cleaning, which for most stoves means emptying ash and brushing the burn pot regularly through the season. On top of that, we recommend a full professional cleaning of the stove's exhaust path and vent once a year, because fine pellet ash builds up where you can't reach.

Who's in there?

The usual chimney guests

An uncapped chimney looks like a hollow tree to a lot of animals. The sound, the time of day and the season usually tell us who it is before we even look.

Chimney swifts

Loud chittering from late spring through summer, often louder when the young are being fed. Federally protected, so timing matters.

Raccoons

Heavy thumping and chattering, often at dusk and night. Mothers commonly use a chimney's smoke shelf to raise kits in spring.

Squirrels

Scratching and scrabbling during the day. Squirrels often fall into a flue and can't climb back out of a smooth liner.

Other birds

Starlings and other birds nest in uncapped flues and dryer and exhaust vents, packing them with twigs and grass.

Don't try to smoke them outLighting a fire under an animal or nest is dangerous. Nest material is very flammable, the smoke may not be able to escape a blocked flue, and a trapped animal can't leave anyway. Keep the damper and fireplace doors closed so nothing gets into the house, and don't try to handle a trapped animal yourself.
Chimney swifts

Protected birds, so we work around their season

Chimney swifts are small, cigar-shaped birds that spend the warm months in North America and winter in South America. They can't perch like most birds, so they cling to the inside of chimneys and build small nests of twigs glued to the flue wall.

Swifts are protected by the federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act. That means an active nest, with eggs or young, can't be removed or disturbed. No reputable sweep will pull them out mid-season.

What we do instead: confirm they're swifts, then schedule the work for after they've left in the fall. Once the chimney is empty, we sweep out the nest material and install a cap so they nest somewhere else next year. Until then, keep the damper closed. It cuts the noise and keeps them in the chimney.

Chimney swift numbers have been declining. If you'd like to leave your chimney open for them, that's a reasonable choice too. Just keep the damper closed and have the chimney swept after they leave, before you use it.

Signs of an animal

What you might notice

  • Chirping, chittering, scratching or thumping from the fireplace or wall
  • Twigs, leaves or nest material falling into the firebox
  • A strong musty or rotten smell from the fireplace
  • Droppings on the smoke shelf, damper or hearth
  • Smoke backing up when you light a fire, from a blocked flue
  • No cap on the chimney, or a damaged one
Bats are differentBats need their own approach and timing, and we'll tell you what applies if that's what we find. Avoid touching a bat or any wild animal with bare hands.

Can you remove the birds in my chimney right now?
It depends on the bird. If they're chimney swifts with an active nest, no: they're protected under the federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act and the nest can't be disturbed until they leave in the fall. We'll schedule the cleanout and cap for then. Other birds and nests can often be dealt with sooner, once we've confirmed what they are.
Why can't I just light a fire to drive them out?
Because it's dangerous and it rarely works. Nests are made of dry, flammable material, a blocked flue can push smoke and carbon monoxide into the house, and young or trapped animals can't escape. Keep the damper closed and call us instead.
How do you get a raccoon out of a chimney?
Humanely, and with timing in mind. In spring a raccoon in the chimney is often a mother with kits, and the goal is to have her move them out together rather than separate them. Once the chimney is empty, we clean out the den material and install a cap so she can't return.
Will a chimney cap keep animals out?
A properly sized, securely fastened cap with intact mesh keeps birds, squirrels and raccoons out of the flue. We check that every flue on the chimney is covered, because animals will find the one that isn't.
The animals are gone. Do I still need the nest removed?
Yes. Nest material blocks the flue, which can push smoke and carbon monoxide back into the house, and it catches fire easily. Droppings and debris also cause odors. Have it cleaned out, and the chimney inspected, before you use the fireplace or any appliance that vents into it.

Why it matters

Your lint screen doesn't catch it all

Every load sends fine lint past the lint screen and into the vent duct. It sticks at bends, at joints and at the outside flap. Over time the duct narrows, the dryer can't push hot, damp air out fast enough, and the drum runs hotter and longer to get the job done.

Lint is very flammable, and a hot dryer with a clogged vent is a common way for laundry room fires to start. NFPA fire data lists failure to clean as the leading factor in home dryer fires. With a gas dryer there's a second risk: a blocked vent can push exhaust, including carbon monoxide, back into the house.

Plenty of Pittsburgh homes have laundry added to a basement, a back room or an upper floor long after the house was built. That often means long duct runs with several bends, or a vent that exits through the roof, which clogs faster and is hard to reach yourself.

Signs it's time

Your dryer is telling you

  • Loads take two cycles, or towels come out damp
  • The top of the dryer or the laundry room gets unusually hot
  • A burning or scorched smell when the dryer runs
  • Lint around the dryer door, behind the dryer or at the vent outside
  • The outside flap barely opens, or you feel little air there while it runs
  • Birds or nesting material at the outside vent
  • It's been more than a year, or never
Burning smell or a very hot dryer?Stop using it, turn it off and unplug it if you can do so safely. If you see smoke or flames, get out and call 911.
What a good vent looks like

Cleaning helps. A good duct helps more.

Some vents clog fast because of how they were installed. While we clean, we check the run against common code requirements and the dryer maker's instructions, and tell you if something is working against you.

Requirements vary with the code your municipality has adopted and with your dryer's installation instructions, which can allow longer runs. We'll tell you what applies to your setup.

DuctSmooth-wall rigid metal, usually 4 inches, with joints taped rather than screwed so screws don't snag lint

How often should a dryer vent be cleaned?
About once a year is a good rule for most homes. Large households, long duct runs with several bends, roof terminations and homes with pets can need it more often. If drying times start creeping up, don't wait for the calendar.
I clean the lint screen every load. Isn't that enough?
It's the most important habit, but the screen doesn't catch everything. Fine lint passes through and builds up in the duct, especially at bends and at the outside flap, where you can't see it.
Can I clean the vent myself?
You can clear the transition duct and the outside flap, and that helps. Cleaning the full length, especially a long run with bends or a roof vent, takes rotary brushes, the right connectors and a way to pull the lint out instead of packing it further down the duct. That's the part we do.
Is the foil or white plastic duct behind my dryer OK?
Plastic and thin foil ducts are generally not acceptable for dryer exhaust: they crush easily, trap lint in their ridges and can't contain a lint fire well. Most dryer makers and model codes call for a listed transition duct, kept short, connected to rigid metal duct. We'll point it out if yours should be replaced.
Can you put a screen on the outside vent to keep birds out?
Not a regular screen. Screens on dryer terminations catch lint and clog quickly, and codes don't allow them. If birds are getting in, a guard designed specifically for dryer exhaust, together with a flap that closes properly, is the safer fix.

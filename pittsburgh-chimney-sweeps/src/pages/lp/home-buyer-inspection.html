What you get

An inspection built for a deadline

NFPA 211, the national chimney safety standard, calls for a Level 2 inspection when a property is sold or transferred. Here's what that includes with us.

Camera down the flue

We run an HD camera through the full length of every flue we inspect, so cracked tiles and gaps aren't guesswork.

Attic, roof and crawlspace

A Level 2 covers the accessible parts of the chimney inside and outside the house, not just the firebox.

Realtor-ready report

A written report with photos and plain-English findings. Easy to forward to the other agent or attach to a repair request.

Scheduled around closing

Tell us your inspection contingency or closing date when you book and we'll work to fit it.

When you need one

Book a Level 2 if

  • You're buying a home with a fireplace, wood stove or chimney-vented furnace
  • Your home inspector wrote "recommend evaluation by a chimney professional"
  • You're selling and want to know what a buyer's inspection will find
  • The seller can't say when the chimney was last swept or inspected
  • You see a cracked crown, missing cap, stained ceiling or loose brick
  • You're a realtor and need a chimney report your clients can rely on

Call and tell us the date. We'll find the earliest opening that works and get the report to everyone who needs it. Saturday appointments available.

Didn't my home inspector already check the chimney?
A general home inspection usually covers what's visible from the outside and the firebox. It doesn't include running a camera through the inside of the flue, which is where cracked liners and gaps tend to hide. That's what a Level 2 inspection adds.
How much does a Level 2 inspection cost?
It depends on how many flues the house has and how easy they are to reach. We confirm the price in writing when you book, before anyone comes out. Repair estimates that come out of the inspection are free.
How fast can you get it done?
Real estate inspections get fast scheduling, because contingency periods are short. Tell us your closing date when you book and we'll find the earliest slot we can. Calling is the quickest way to lock one in.
Who can receive the report?
Anyone you name. Buyers, sellers and agents often all want a copy. List their emails in the notes when you book so the report reaches everyone who needs it.
Does the seller need to be home?
Someone needs to let us in and give us access to the fireplace, attic and basement. Most buyers' agents arrange this the same way they do for the home inspection.

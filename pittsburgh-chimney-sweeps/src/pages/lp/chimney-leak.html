Signs of a chimney leak

Don't wait for the ceiling to go

  • Brown or yellow water stains on the ceiling or wall next to the chimney
  • Dripping or standing water in the firebox after rain
  • A musty, damp smell from the fireplace, worse in wet weather
  • Peeling paint or bubbling drywall around the chimney breast
  • White staining (efflorescence) or flaking brick on the outside
  • A rusted damper or rust streaks in the firebox

Pittsburgh's freeze-thaw winters make leaks worse over time: water that gets into masonry freezes, expands and opens the gaps further.

How much does a chimney leak repair cost?
It depends entirely on the cause. A loose cap and a failed flashing job are very different repairs. That's why we diagnose first, then give you a free written estimate with photos before any work begins.
Couldn't it just be my roof?
Sometimes it is. But water that shows up near a chimney often comes from the chimney itself: the crown, the cap, the brick or the flashing where chimney meets roof. We check all of it and tell you honestly what we find.
Will you need to come inside?
Usually, yes. We look at the stain, the firebox and the attic where we can reach it, as well as the outside of the chimney. Drop cloths and shoe covers go down first.
Can you fix it in the winter?
Many leak repairs can be done in cold weather, and some masonry work needs a stretch of milder temperatures. We'll tell you which applies to your chimney when we give you the estimate.
Should I keep using my fireplace?
If water is getting into the chimney, hold off until we've looked at it. Moisture can damage the liner and masonry in ways you can't see from the firebox.

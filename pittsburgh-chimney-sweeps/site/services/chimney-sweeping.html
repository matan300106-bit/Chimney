Why it matters

What a sweep actually removes

Every fire sends smoke up the flue. As that smoke cools, it leaves behind soot and creosote, a tarry residue that sticks to the flue walls. Creosote is combustible. Enough of it, plus a hot fire, is how most chimney fires start.

Buildup also narrows the flue. A narrower flue drafts poorly, so smoke lingers in the firebox or rolls into the room, and the fire burns cooler and dirtier, which makes even more creosote. A sweep breaks that cycle.

Pittsburgh's long heating season means a lot of burning between October and April. Many of the city's older homes also have chimneys that serve a furnace or water heater alongside the fireplace. We check every flue in the chimney, not just the one you burn in, because a blocked appliance flue can push carbon monoxide back into the house.

What's included

Every sweep, every time

  • Home protection first. Drop cloths from your door to the hearth, shoe covers, and a HEPA vacuum that keeps the firebox under negative pressure.
  • Full-length flue cleaning. Brushes or rotary tools sized to your flue. Wire for clay tile, poly heads for stainless liners so we don't scratch them.
  • Smoke chamber and smoke shelf. The area right above the damper collects the heaviest debris and is easy to miss.
  • Firebox, damper and ash. Cleaned, and the damper checked for a full open and close.
  • Level 1 inspection. Firebox, damper, visible flue, crown, cap and exterior brick, plus clearances to anything combustible.
  • Photo report. Before-and-after photos and anything we noticed, texted to you the same day.
Signs it's time

When to call a sweep

  • It's been more than a year, or you don't know when it was last swept
  • Smoke drifts into the room, or fires are hard to start and keep going
  • A strong campfire smell from the fireplace, especially on humid days
  • Black, flaky or shiny deposits on the damper or the flue walls
  • Debris, twigs or soot falling into the firebox
  • You burned a lot of softwood, wet wood or long, smouldering fires
  • You just bought the house and have no service history
Heard a loud roar or saw flames from the top?That can be a chimney fire. Get everyone out and call 911. Don't use the fireplace again until it has been inspected, ideally with a Level 2 camera inspection.
Creosote, explained

Three stages, three different jobs

Chimney sweeps group creosote into three stages. The stage tells us how to remove it and tells you something about how your fireplace is burning.

Stage 1

Soot and dry flakes

Fine, powdery or flaky deposits. Normal for a fireplace that burns hot with dry wood. A standard brush removes it completely.

Stage 2

Crunchy, tar-like flakes

Shiny black granules that stick harder. Often a sign of restricted air or cooler fires. Usually removed with rotary tools.

Stage 3

Glazed creosote

A hard, shiny coating that looks like poured tar. It's highly flammable and brushing alone won't remove it. It needs chemical treatment or specialised rotary work, sometimes over several visits.

CSIA guidance is to sweep a wood-burning flue once about 1/8 inch of sooty buildup has formed, and sooner if any glazed creosote is present. More on creosote stages

How often

It depends on what you burn

FAQ

Chimney sweeping questions

Something else on your mind? Call or text and talk to a sweep.

(412) 555-0142
Will sweeping my chimney make a mess in the house?
It shouldn't. We put down drop cloths from the door to the hearth, wear shoe covers, and seal the fireplace opening while a HEPA vacuum keeps the firebox under negative pressure. Soot gets pulled into the vacuum instead of drifting into the room.
Do you have to go on the roof?
Not always. Many chimneys can be swept from the firebox. We still check the crown and cap from the roof when it's safe to do so, because that's where a lot of water damage starts. Steep or icy roofs are assessed on the day.
I only have a gas fireplace. Does it need sweeping?
Gas doesn't make creosote, so it rarely needs a full sweep. It does still need an annual inspection. Nests, fallen debris and flaking flue tiles can block a gas vent and push carbon monoxide into the house. See our gas fireplace service.
Can a sweep remove glazed (stage 3) creosote?
Usually, but not in one ordinary brushing. Glazed creosote needs chemical treatment, specialised rotary tools, or both, and sometimes more than one visit. If the glaze is severe or the flue is damaged, relining can be the better long-term fix. We'll show you photos and explain the options.
What if you find a problem during the sweep?
You'll see it in the photo report with a plain explanation of how urgent it is. Repairs get a free written estimate. Nothing is done without your OK, and we won't pressure you to decide on the spot.

Maintenance guide

Does a gas fireplace need maintenance?

Yes. Gas burns cleaner than wood, but a gas fireplace and its vent should still be inspected once a year. The main risks aren't soot. They're blocked vents, misplaced logs, worn parts and carbon monoxide.

Why gas fireplaces need a yearly check

It's easy to think of a gas fireplace as an appliance you can switch on and forget. In daily use it mostly is. But NFPA 211, the national standard for chimneys, fireplaces and vents, calls for an annual inspection whatever the fuel, and most gas fireplace manufacturers call for yearly service by a qualified technician in the owner's manual.

The reason is venting. A gas fireplace makes carbon monoxide, water vapor and other combustion gases, just as a furnace does. As long as the vent is clear, sealed and the burner is set up correctly, those gases go outside. If a bird nests in the vent, a joint separates, the logs get moved or the glass seal fails, they can come back into the room.

Know which kind you have

"Gas fireplace" covers several quite different appliances. The owner's manual or the label near the controls will tell you which one you have.

What a yearly service covers

Exactly what's checked depends on the unit and its manual, but a good gas fireplace service looks at the whole system:

  • Vent and termination: clear of nests, debris and plants, joints sealed, cap undamaged.
  • Chimney and liner, for gas logs or inserts in a masonry fireplace.
  • Glass and gasket: no cracks, seated properly, cleaned with the right product.
  • Logs and embers placed exactly as the manufacturer's diagram shows.
  • Burner, pilot and ignition: cleaned, lighting reliably, flame looking right.
  • Gas connections checked for leaks.
  • Damper fixed open where required, for vented gas logs.
  • Carbon monoxide alarm nearby and working.

What you can safely do yourself

  • Keep the outside vent clear. After a heavy snow, check that drifts haven't covered a wall-mounted cap, and keep shrubs and mulch away from it.
  • Test your CO alarms monthly and replace them on the schedule printed on the alarm.
  • Clean the glass only when it's cold, using the cleaner the manufacturer recommends. A light white haze is a normal combustion deposit. Ordinary household glass cleaners aren't made for it and can damage some fireplace glass.
  • Dust the vents and controls around the unit, and keep the area in front of it clear.
  • Keep the safety screen on. The glass on a gas fireplace gets hot enough to cause serious burns, which is why newer units come with a mesh barrier.

Leave gas lines, valves, burners and log placement to a qualified technician. Moving logs even slightly can make the flame hit them, which creates soot and can increase carbon monoxide.

Warning signs to act on

Turn the fireplace off and have it looked at if you notice:

  • Black soot on the glass, the logs or the wall above the opening
  • A change in the flame: much taller, lazier, lifting off the burner or very different in color from what it used to be
  • A pilot light that keeps going out, or a fireplace that won't light
  • Condensation running down the inside of the glass after the first few minutes
  • A smell that lingers beyond the brief "new" smell some units give off at the start of the season
  • Your carbon monoxide alarm going off, even once
Smell gas, or CO alarm sounding?If you smell gas (a rotten-egg smell), leave the house right away. Don't switch lights or appliances on or off, and call 911 or your gas utility once you're outside. If your carbon monoxide alarm sounds or people feel unwell with headaches, dizziness or nausea, get everyone into fresh air and call 911.

Gas logs in an old masonry fireplace

Many Pittsburgh homes have a gas log set in what was once a wood-burning fireplace. Two things matter here. First, vented gas logs need the damper fixed partly or fully open, usually with a clamp, so it can't be closed while the logs are on. Second, the chimney above them is still a chimney. It still needs a yearly inspection for nests, crumbling tile and water damage, and it may need a liner sized for gas. Gas exhaust is full of water vapor, and in a cold, oversized flue it condenses and slowly breaks down old mortar and tile. More on that is in our guide to chimney leak signs.

A word on vent-free units

Vent-free logs and fireplaces release their combustion gases and moisture into the room. They're designed with oxygen-depletion sensors and limits on room size and run time, and they have to be used exactly as the manual says. If you have one, be strict about the manual, keep a CO alarm nearby, and have the unit serviced every year. If you're choosing a new unit, a vented model avoids the question.

The bottom line

  • Inspect a gas fireplace every year, along with its vent or chimney.
  • Keep the outside cap clear, especially after snow.
  • Soot, flame changes and condensation are reasons to call, not to wait.
  • Have working CO alarms and leave gas parts to a qualified technician.

Common questions

How often should a gas fireplace be serviced?
Once a year. NFPA 211 calls for annual inspection of chimneys and vents whatever the fuel, and most manufacturers call for yearly service in the owner's manual. Before the heating season is the most convenient time.
Does a gas fireplace chimney need to be swept?
Not usually in the way a wood-burning chimney does, because gas leaves little soot. It still needs an inspection for nests, debris and liner damage. If something is found, it's cleaned or repaired as needed.
Why is there a white film on my gas fireplace glass?
A light white or cloudy film is a normal deposit from burning gas. Clean it when the glass is cold, using the cleaner your manufacturer recommends. If the film comes back quickly or turns black, have the unit checked, since that can point to a burner or log placement problem.
Can I leave my gas fireplace on all night?
Follow the owner's manual for your model. Many vented units are designed for long run times, but vent-free units often have limits. Never run any gas appliance with the glass cracked or removed, and keep a working carbon monoxide alarm nearby.

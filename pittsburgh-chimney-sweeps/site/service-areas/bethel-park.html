Chimneys in Bethel Park

Post-war homes, hard-working chimneys

Bethel Park is one of the larger South Hills communities, and most of its neighborhoods were built out in the decades after the Second World War. Drive through and you'll see brick ranches, split-levels and two-story homes on gently rolling streets, with South Park just next door.

The typical chimney here is a brick stack that does two jobs. One flue serves a wood-burning fireplace, often in a lower-level family room. Another vents the gas furnace and water heater. Both have been at it for 50 years or more.

That age shows up in a few predictable places. Clay liners crack. Mortar joints at the top of the stack open up. And the furnace flue, which runs every day all winter, collects debris and can be damaged by the moisture in gas exhaust.

Why lower-level fireplaces smoke

In winter, warm air rises through your house and leaks out the top. That pulls air in low, sometimes down the chimney of a basement or lower-level fireplace. The fire ends up fighting the house. A short chimney, a cold flue or a tight, newly sealed home can make it worse.

We check the flue size, chimney height and damper, then tell you what will actually help.

The 3-2-10 ruleBuilding codes generally call for a masonry chimney to rise at least 3 feet above where it leaves the roof, and 2 feet higher than anything within 10 feet. Low ranch chimneys sometimes fall short, which can hurt draft.

Bethel Park FAQ

What neighbors ask us

Have a different question? Call or text and a real person will answer.

Do you service gas furnace flues in Bethel Park?
Yes. In a lot of Bethel Park homes the furnace and water heater vent into a flue in the same brick chimney as the fireplace. We check that flue for blockages, nests, fallen tile and moisture damage, because a blocked appliance flue can push carbon monoxide back into the house.
Our lower-level fireplace smokes when we light it. Can you fix that?
Often, yes. Common causes are a flue that's too cold or too small for the firebox, a chimney that's too short, a partly closed damper, or the house pulling air down the chimney. We'll find the cause first. Fixes range from simple changes in how you start the fire to a taller chimney or a smaller firebox opening.
Our dryer is in the basement. Does the vent still need cleaning?
Yes. Short runs still collect lint, and the flexible foil duct behind many dryers gets crushed against the wall. We clean the full run, replace damaged duct if you want, and test airflow at the outside hood.

Chimneys in Upper St. Clair

Big chimneys, built in the suburban boom

Upper St. Clair grew mostly after the Second World War, as the South Hills filled in with single-family neighborhoods. A lot of the homes here are two-story colonials and other traditional styles, built from the 1950s on, often with a full-height brick chimney on an outside wall.

It's common to find two fireplaces, one in the living room and one in a family room or basement, plus a furnace or water-heater flue, all in the same stack. More flues means more to inspect, and a cap that has to cover all of them.

An exterior chimney that rises two stories takes the full force of wind, rain and freeze-thaw. The first thing to fail is usually the crown, the concrete slab at the very top. Many chimneys from this era were finished with a thin layer of mortar instead of a properly formed crown. It cracks, water gets into the brick below, and the damage spreads down the stack.

Upper St. Clair FAQ

Local questions, straight answers

Everything else is on our main FAQ.

We have two fireplaces in one chimney. Do both need sweeping?
Each flue is its own passage, so each one gets cleaned and inspected on its own. If one fireplace hardly gets used, it may need less cleaning, but it still needs an inspection for nests, debris and liner damage. We'll note every flue separately in your photo report.
Can you inspect the chimney before we close on a house in Upper St. Clair?
Yes. NFPA 211 calls for a Level 2 inspection at a property sale, which includes a camera look inside every flue. We schedule around closing dates and send a written report your agent can share. More on our real estate page.
Is a cracked crown worth fixing, or can it wait?
Small hairline cracks can sometimes be sealed. Wide cracks, missing chunks or a flat mortar wash usually mean the crown should be rebuilt. Waiting lets water into the brick each winter, which turns a crown job into masonry repair. We'll show you photos and give you a written price for each option.
We switched our fireplace to gas logs. Does it still need service?
Yes. Gas logs still need a yearly inspection of the flue, damper and logs. We also check that the damper is set correctly for vented gas logs, since a closed damper with gas logs burning is a carbon monoxide hazard.

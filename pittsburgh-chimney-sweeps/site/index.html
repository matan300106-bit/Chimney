Know before you burn

Inspection levels, explained plainly

The national standard (NFPA 211) defines three levels of chimney inspection. We'll tell you which one you need and why, so you never pay for one you don't.

Annual check-up

Best for: yearly maintenance, no changes to your system

A visual check of the readily accessible parts of the chimney, flue and appliance. It comes free with every sweep.

  • Firebox, damper & smoke chamber
  • Creosote level & blockages
  • Exterior crown, cap & brick
  • Clearances to combustibles

Camera inspection

Best for: buying/selling a home, after a chimney fire, storm or new appliance

Everything in Level 1, plus an HD video scan of the whole flue and access to attics and crawlspaces. NFPA 211 calls for it when a property is sold or transferred.

  • Full-length HD flue video
  • Cracked tiles & liner gaps
  • Attic & roofline check
  • Realtor-ready written report

Concealed-area inspection

Best for: when a Level 2 points to a hidden hazard

Only needed when serious damage is suspected behind walls or masonry. Some components may need removal to access. We'll always explain why before recommending it.

  • Access to concealed areas
  • Structural assessment
  • Detailed repair scope
  • Rarely needed, never upsold

Built for Pittsburgh homes

Hills, old brick and hard winters

Pittsburgh chimneys take a beating. A lot of the housing stock here is older brick, and our winters swing above and below freezing again and again. Water gets into mortar, freezes, expands, and slowly pushes brick apart.

  • Freeze-thaw damage. We look for spalling brick, cracked crowns and open mortar joints before they turn into a rebuild.
  • Older clay-tile flues. Many older homes have clay liners that crack with age. Our camera shows you exactly what's inside.
  • Steep roofs and hillside lots. Our crews are equipped and insured for tall chimneys and tricky access.
  • Furnace and water-heater flues. Gas appliances vent through chimneys too, and a blocked flue is a carbon monoxide risk.

How often should my chimney be swept?
NFPA 211, the national chimney safety standard, calls for an inspection every year and cleaning as needed. Most wood-burning fireplaces used regularly need a sweep annually. Gas fireplaces still need an annual inspection, since nests, debris and deteriorating liners can cause carbon monoxide problems.
How long does a sweep take?
Usually 45–90 minutes for a standard sweep and Level 1 inspection. Camera inspections and multi-flue chimneys take a little longer. We'll give you an estimate when you book.
Will it make a mess in my house?
No. We lay drop cloths from the door to the fireplace, wear shoe covers, and run a HEPA vacuum system that keeps soot under negative pressure the whole time.
I'm buying a house. Do I need a Level 2 inspection?
NFPA 211 calls for a Level 2 inspection on property sale or transfer. A general home inspection usually doesn't look inside the flue. A Level 2 camera inspection shows you its condition before you close.
Do you give free estimates on repairs?
Yes. If we find something during a sweep or inspection, you'll get a written, itemized estimate with photos at no extra charge and no pressure to decide on the spot.
